B2B SaaS Pricing Strategy

Yesterday's lesson said Net Revenue Retention is the clearest proof a growth loop is compounding on its own. Today is the decision that actually produces that number: how you price and package the product decides whether expansion happens automatically as customers succeed, or has to be re-won by hand at every renewal.

Yesterday you learned that retention is the multiplier on every growth loop, and that NRR above 100% is the clearest sign a B2B business is compounding without new logos. Today is the lever that makes that possible in the first place: pricing and packaging decide whether a customer's growing success turns into growing revenue by itself, or whether every bit of expansion has to be negotiated into existence.
01

The Big Idea

Most pricing mistakes aren't about the number — they're about the unit the number is attached to. That unit is the value metric: the thing you charge per, whether that's a seat, a thousand API calls, a contact in a database, or a message sent. A price built on the wrong value metric is disconnected from the value the customer actually gets, which means two customers getting wildly different amounts of value can end up paying the same amount — and a customer who grows ten times over gets ten times the benefit while paying the company nothing more for it.

Packaging is the second half of the decision: how features and capability are bundled into tiers, usually some version of Good · Better · Best. Good packaging makes the upgrade path obvious exactly when a customer is outgrowing their tier — the gate they hit is the capability they actually need next. Bad packaging gates arbitrary features behind arbitrary tiers, so an upgrade conversation feels like a negotiation instead of something the customer already expected. Pricing and packaging together are what make land-and-expand work: price low enough to win the first use case, then let usage growth or tier upgrades carry the account upward without a renegotiation at every step.

Per-Seat Does value scale with how many people are logged in?

Price tracks headcount using the product. Clean to quote, but flat if usage per seat varies wildly — a power user and a casual one cost the same.

Usage-Based Does value scale with volume consumed, not headcount?

Price tracks a consumption unit — API calls, contacts, messages sent, data processed. Expansion happens automatically as the customer's own activity grows.

Flat-Rate / Tiered Is value mostly about which capabilities you can access?

A fixed price per tier bundles a set of features. Simple and predictable, but blind to how intensively a customer actually uses what's inside the tier.

Hybrid (Platform + Usage) Do you need a revenue floor plus upside as usage grows?

A base platform fee covers access and implementation cost; a usage component on top captures expansion as the account scales.

  • Price the value metric, not the feature count. The unit you charge on should move in the same direction as the value the customer receives — if it doesn't, pricing and value have quietly come apart.
  • Packaging's job is to make the next tier obvious, not to gate arbitrary features. A customer should hit a wall exactly where they actually need more capability, never somewhere unrelated to why they're growing.
  • Land low enough to win the first use case; expand through usage growth, not renegotiation. The best expansion revenue is the kind that shows up on the invoice automatically because the customer is succeeding.
  • A pricing model that doesn't move with customer success is a growth loop with nothing feeding it. It's the same leak Day 26 described — except here the leak isn't churn, it's value the company delivered and never captured.
— Feature-Gated Packaging "Our Enterprise tier unlocks SSO, custom branding, and API access for $50K a year flat — same price whether you send 10K or 10M messages."

Price is disconnected from the value metric. A customer who scales usage twenty-fold pays exactly what they paid on day one, so expansion revenue has nowhere to come from except a renewal renegotiation.

What happens: growth stalls between renewals; every bit of expansion has to be argued for instead of arriving on its own.

+ Value-Metric Packaging "We charge a platform fee plus a per-message rate, with volume breakpoints that unlock orchestration automatically as a customer's program grows."

Price tracks the value metric directly. As a customer's campaigns scale, so does revenue — without a single upsell call.

What happens: expansion revenue compounds on its own, feeding exactly the NRR loop from yesterday's lesson.

Pricing is a product decision, not a late finance exercise

A PM who treats pricing and packaging as something finance bolts on near launch ships a product that under-monetizes its best customers and over-charges its smallest ones. The value metric is as much a product design choice as any feature on the roadmap — it belongs in discovery, not in a spreadsheet after the fact.

02

Worked Example

B2B SaaS Scenario

A B2B SaaS company is evaluating whether to reprice from three flat per-seat tiers to usage-based pricing metered on monthly active API calls. Here's how the two compare.

DimensionPer-seat (current)Usage-based on contacts (proposed)
Revenue vs. customer valueWeak — a 3-person team managing 50K contacts pays the same as a 3-person team managing 2MStrong — price scales directly with contacts actively marketed to
Expansion triggerRequires adding headcount, or a renewal negotiationHappens automatically as the customer's contact base grows
Sales cycle impactSimple to quote, but customers negotiate seat count downSlightly more setup, but anchors price to ROI the customer already sees
Main riskCustomers maximize seats-per-dollar by sharing loginsCustomers may dispute what counts as "active"; needs clean usage metering
Best fitEarly-stage, low usage variance across customersPlatforms where usage varies hugely customer to customer

The catch: usage-based pricing only works if the value metric is something customers already track and trust. If "active contacts marketed to" feels ambiguous or manipulable, customers will dispute the bill every month instead of seeing it as fair — the metering has to be as transparent as the pricing itself.

Scenario Drill

The situation Your platform prices on three flat tiers (Starter, Growth, Enterprise) gated by features — number of channels, number of integrations, SSO. None of the tiers meter contact volume. Before your next pricing review:
  • Your biggest Growth-tier customer has scaled their active contact list from 200K to 4M in 18 months, with zero price increase, since the tier doesn't meter contacts.
  • Customer Success reports this account is now your single heaviest infrastructure consumer — it costs more to serve than it pays.
  • Sales says "don't touch pricing on this account, we'll lose them if we try to charge more."
Identify what's wrong with the current value metric, propose how you'd fix pricing and packaging going forward for new and existing customers without triggering a renewal crisis, and write the one question you'd ask Sales before any conversation with the customer.

The assignment to avoid

  • Quietly accepting the margin erosion because "we'll lose them if we try to charge more" is a sales objection to a conversation that hasn't happened yet, not evidence from one that has.

What's wrong with the value metric

  • The tier gates features, not usage, so it's disconnected from the actual cost and value driver: contact volume. A customer can scale usage twenty-fold with zero corresponding price increase — the company is quietly subsidizing its best customer's growth.

The fix going forward

  • Introduce a usage-based component, priced per contact-volume band, on all new contracts and new tiers from day one. Grandfather existing customers at current terms through the life of their current contract — don't open with a surprise price increase mid-term.
  • Walk the next renewal in with a value story, not an invoice: show the account what they've achieved going from 200K to 4M contacts, then introduce the new metered structure as the model that scales with them going forward, not a penalty for having grown.

The question for Sales

  • "Have we actually asked this customer how the platform's value to them has changed since they went from 200K to 4M contacts — or are we assuming they'd leave before having that conversation?"
"We don't fix this by raising the existing customer's price overnight — we fix it by making every new contract scale with contacts from day one, and by walking this renewal in with a value story instead of a bill."
MEETING MOVE

Use this in your next pricing or packaging review

Redirect the conversation from the price point to the metric underneath it:

"What's our value metric here, and does it move in the same direction as the value the customer is getting — or did we pick it because it was the easiest thing to bill?"
05

Glossary

Value Metric (Pricing Metric)
The unit a company charges on — seats, contacts, API calls, messages sent — that should scale with the value delivered to the customer. Widely considered the single most consequential pricing decision in B2B SaaS.
Packaging
How features and capabilities are bundled into tiers, commonly Good · Better · Best, designed so that upgrading happens naturally when a customer needs more value, not through arbitrary feature gates.
Land and Expand
A go-to-market motion that prices an initial, low-friction use case to win the account, then grows revenue over time through usage growth, seat growth, or tier upgrades rather than one large upfront deal.
Willingness to Pay (WTP)
The maximum price a customer segment will accept for a given level of value, usually estimated through customer interviews, van Westendorp pricing surveys, or competitive anchoring — the ceiling good packaging is designed to approach without crossing.
Price Anchoring
Presenting a higher-priced option, even one few customers choose, alongside others so the middle tier reads as the reasonable, expected choice — a packaging tactic, not a pricing metric.
